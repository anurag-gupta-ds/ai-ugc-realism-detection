InterpretationWhat This Pilot Proves

  • The full pipeline — extraction → features → labeling → training → evaluation — runs end-to-end without breaking.
  • XGBoost performs solidly even at small scale (94 samples): 84.2% of predictions landed within ±1 point of the human-assigned realism score.
  • GNN underperforms at this scale (R² = 0.038), which is expected — graph neural networks are data-hungry and typically need substantially more samples to learn meaningful structure. This does not invalidate the GNN approach; it confirms the plan to scale to the full 2,000-video dataset before drawing conclusions.
  • The dominant feature importance from detection-rate columns (face/pose visibility) indicates the pilot's realism labels partly reflect video clarity rather than pure movement naturalism — a labeling-rubric issue identified and being corrected ahead of full-scale rollout.
Bottom line: The pipeline is validated and functional. XGBoost is the stronger performer at pilot scale and is confirmed as the baseline model. GNN's weak pilot result is consistent with its known data requirements and is not a disqualifying signal — full-scale training on 2,000 videos is the next test of its actual capability.

Next StepsBefore Scaling to 2,000 Videos

  1. Correct the Label Studio rubric to rate movement/expression naturalism rather than visibility, and re-score the pilot labels.
  2. Exclude face_detection_rate, pose_detection_rate, and has_face_data from the primary feature set, or explicitly flag them as pipeline-quality indicators rather than realism signal.
  3. Re-run correlation and importance checks on the corrected labels to confirm behavioral features (blink rate, mouth movement, motion) carry the predictive signal.
  4. Scale frame extraction, MediaPipe feature extraction, and labeling to the full 2,000-video dataset.
  5. Retrain both models on the full dataset — GNN performance is expected to improve materially with volume.
